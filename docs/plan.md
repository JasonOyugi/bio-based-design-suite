# Bio-based Systems Explorer: concept and plan

Oct 27, 2026 · Jason Oyugi

## The brief, restated

Design an interactive system that makes the building systems our pilots are testing legible, comparable and actionable for the people who decide whether bio-based construction gets specified, financed, made and approved in East Africa.

The brief as I have read it, so the team can correct it:

- **Inspiration.** BASE's [CBFT Design Suite](https://base-builds.com/base-innovation-center/cbft-design-suite/) shows one proven bamboo system in an interactive, guided way. We want the equivalent for our portfolio, but it must serve our strategy, not copy BASE's.
- **Content.** The construction systems we are testing, starting with bio-based floors, shown only within what the regional value chain can actually deliver. Imported or exported routes are allowed, provided the economics are run honestly.
- **Audience.** Developers, manufacturers, engineers, architects and others. Who matters most, and how to reach them, has to be worked out by benchmarking tools that have actually moved specification elsewhere.
- **Output.** A grounded plan: what to build, for whom, in what order, and how it plugs into the proof engine and pull engine of the programme strategy.

## The recommendation in one page

Build an evidence-graded catalogue and comparator of bio-based building systems, not a design generator, and start with floors because four of our five pilots touch a floor and the SHOUT/Zima work already has seven options scored against a baseline.

- **Why not copy BASE.** BASE built its design tool on one proprietary system that is already accredited under the Philippines' AITECH scheme and has been used in more than 1,500 homes. Our systems are still in discovery, so a tool that generates designs or structural reports would claim a certainty we do not have.
- **What works elsewhere.** The tools that actually changed specification share three traits: verified build-up data that authorities accept ([dataholz.eu](https://www.holzforschung.at/en/knowledge-transfer/online-platforms/)), free human project support with tools as the hook ([WoodWorks](https://www.woodworks.org/contact/)), and a standard cost or carbon method that practitioners trust and contribute to ([CAHF](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/library/benchmarking-housing-construction-costs-in-africa/), [EC3](https://www.buildingtransparency.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/BT_FLIPBOOK_GREENBUILD.pdf)).
- **Who it is for.** The front door is the developer's development manager and their quantity surveyor (QS), because they make the cost-and-risk call on incremental adoption. Engineers get datasheet depth behind it. Manufacturers get a demand and specification view. Lenders, valuers, insurers and approving authorities get exported evidence packs, not a separate app.
- **What makes it ours.** Every number carries its evidence grade (judgement, prototype, lab, live project, repeat project), and every system shows its weakest of the five readiness tests. That puts the strategy's multiplication rule, where one failing factor sinks the system, on screen.
- **How we judge it.** By decision events, not users: specifications that cite a catalogue build-up, supplier quotes against a catalogue spec, approvals or lender decisions that reuse an evidence pack.
- **First step.** A two-month discovery sprint (November to December 2026) that defines the data schema, loads the seven floor options, and tests a clickable prototype with about ten users before any build spend.

## What our strategy says the system must do

The system has to make the programme's own decision model visible: viability is technical integration × repeatable manufacturing × financeability × market acceptance, filtered through codes, and a pathway that fails one factor fails.

| Strategy element (Strategy & Learning Framework, Oct 2026) | What the system must therefore do |
| --- | --- |
| Five readiness tests, multiplied | Score every system on all five and show the weakest link first, not an average |
| Evidence tracks P (pilot), E (external), S (engineered signal) | Tag every number with its source and grade; never show a single point estimate without its range |
| Platform vs incremental developers | Serve incremental developers best: they need comparable, low-friction component choices. Platform developers need hands-on support, not a tool |
| "Lead with clear, accessible data" and "end over-specification with regional grading" | Specify by regional species and grade (e.g. Eucalyptus grandis D30, kiln-dried pine) and show what happens if a batch grades lower |
| Components first, structure later | Start with floors and partitions; add structural frames as pilots mature |
| Pull engine paced to evidence (EDGE/IFC, lenders, insurers, valuers) | Export a per-system evidence pack in the format each institution asks for, starting with MAGC-compatible LCA data |
| Stop triggers written in advance | Show a system's status honestly, including "paused" or "stopped" with the binding constraint, so the tool publishes negative results too |
| Region as one value chain (Uganda and mainland Tanzania as resource base; Kenya, Rwanda, Zanzibar as end markets) | Price systems on landed cost by route, not by country of use alone |

The regulatory review sets the realism bar: none of the four countries prohibits timber, bamboo or earth, and none yet offers a routine pre-approved pathway. Early projects should be packaged as evidence-led approvals, which is exactly what an evidence pack can carry.

## Benchmarks: what works elsewhere and why

The tools that shift what gets built pair verified data with a route to a real decision (an approval, a quote, a loan); interactive design on its own is the least important ingredient.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [CBFT Design Suite](https://base-builds.com/base-innovation-center/cbft-design-suite/) | BASE Bahay Foundation (Hilti Foundation) | Early-stage designers of CBFT houses | Guided configuration of one bamboo system; outputs are exploratory and explicitly not for construction | Built on a system [accredited under AITECH](https://kanto.ph/spaces/base-bahay/) and used in [1,500+ homes](https://kanto.ph/?p=21899) | The tool followed accreditation and volume. We have neither yet, so we should not generate designs |
| [dataholz.eu](https://www.holzforschung.at/en/knowledge-transfer/online-platforms/) | Holzforschung Austria with proHolz | Planners, contractors, owners, building authorities | Catalogue of timber build-ups with fire, acoustic, thermal and ecological data checked by accredited bodies | [Accepted by building authorities in Austria and, since 2018, Germany; about 5.1m page views a year](https://www.wko.at/ooe/gewerbe-handwerk/holzbau/vortrag-bettina-ploessnig-weigel--dataholz.eu.pdf) | Authority-accepted build-ups end project-by-project proof. This is our "repeatable approval" condition in product form |

Our cost engine should speak it |
| [EDGE App](https://edgebuildings.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/IFC0060-EDGE-Brochure-Kenya-2021_FIN_LR-Reference.pdf) | IFC | Developers, designers, lenders | Free design software with certification linked to green finance | [212 certified buildings in Kenya by May 2025](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/sports/amp/real-estate/2001518351/kenya-attains-key-milestone-in-sustainable-buildings) | Finance linkage drives use. Feed EDGE's materials module; do not compete with it |
| [WikiHouse](https://wikihouse.cc) | Open Systems Lab | Self-builders, small developers, local fabricators | Open-source timber cassette system cut by a distributed network of local fabricators | Skylark tested at full scale at the [BRE](https://awaengineers.com/?p=1731) | An open specification plus a fabricator network spreads supply. Engineers only sign off once behaviour is characterised by tests |

Four lessons carry into the design. Verified, authority-facing data beats a slick configurator. A human support desk behind the tool converts projects. Cost must be in the QS's own BOQ language. And the host must be neutral and durable, or the data goes stale when the grant ends.

## Who to target, and how

Target the specification decision in developer-built mid-rise first: the developer's development manager and QS decide, the structural engineer signs off, and the architect proposes. Everyone else is served through what the tool exports.

| Priority | Audience | Decision they make | What they need from the system | How we reach them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Incremental developers' development managers (e.g. Zima/SHOUT, CrossBoundary/Mosaic, Acorn) | Whether a component goes on the spec list for the next block | Installed cost per m² vs their incumbent, programme time, who captures the saving, risk status | Direct, through pilots and a support desk; the tool is the follow-up, not the cold open |
| 1 | Their QS | Whether the cost case is real | BOQ-format line items, ranges, sensitivities, landed material cost by route | Kenyan QS bodies and the QS on each pilot; CAHF-style standard BOQ |
| 2 | Structural and fire engineers (e.g. Studio III, Studio OMT, D7 Engenharia) | Whether they can sign off | Build-up datasheets, design basis, test references, code route per country | Datasheet layer; CPD sessions with engineering institutions |
| 2 | Manufacturers and processors (e.g. Timber World, Busoga Forestry, Ugalumbers, MoKo) | Whether to invest in kilns, grading or a line | Aggregated demand by spec and volume, target grade, moisture and tolerance, price to beat | Supplier profiles and a demand view; the pull side of the tool |
| 3 | Architects | Whether to propose it | Build-up options that fit familiar finishes and depths | Through developers and design awards later; not the first door |
| 3 | Lenders, valuers, insurers (e.g. KMRC, IHS Fund II, EDGE teams at IFC) | Whether to lend, value or cover on ordinary terms | A standard evidence pack per system, EDGE materials result | Exported packs, paced to evidence as the strategy requires |
| 3 | Approving authorities (NCA, county planning, Rwanda's BPMIS, ZIPA) | Whether to approve without exception | A reusable approval dossier per build-up | Exported dossiers; later, recognition of catalogue build-ups |
| Out of scope | Self-builders and incremental households | n/a | n/a | The strategy excludes self-build for now; the MicroPlots catalogue idea is the only bridge worth watching |

This ranking is a hypothesis from our documents and the benchmarks. The discovery sprint should test it with about ten interviews: two developers, two QS, two engineers, two manufacturers, one lender and one approving authority.

## The proposed system: five layers

The system is one graded evidence base with a readiness scorecard on top, three front doors for the people who specify, sign off and supply, and exports for the institutions the pull engine targets.

Diagram (described in text; the original is in the shared doc), read bottom up:

1. **Evidence base** (every number graded P, E or S): build-ups (spec + tests), cost engine (BOQ, routes), LCA data (MAGC format), suppliers (grade, capacity), approvals (route by country). Fed by pilot data (P), labs and benchmarks (E), supplier quotes (S) and contributed BOQs.
2. **Readiness scorecard**: five tests per system (technical, supply, finance, acceptance, codes), weakest shown first.
3. **Front door: developer and QS comparator** (the main front door): cost vs incumbent, who captures the benefit. Plus **engineer datasheets**: build-ups, test references, code route by country.
4. **Market-making: manufacturer view**: demand by spec and grade, and the price to beat.
5. **Institutional exports**: finance pack for lenders, valuers and insurers; approval dossier per build-up for NCA, counties, BPMIS and ZIPA; EDGE and MAGC export of materials and LCA data in IFC formats.

It can start light: a structured dataset behind a simple web front end, so sprint money goes to data and testing with users rather than software. A working title such as "Bio-based Systems Explorer" is a placeholder for the team to replace.

## First module: floors

Floors go first because Bauhaus Earth named floor and ceiling systems as the clearest cost and speed case, the SHOUT/Zima pre-screen already scores seven options against the beam-and-block baseline, and floor decisions also sit inside the ADHI, CPS and CrossBoundary pilots.

**What a user puts in.** Country and city, storeys (2 to 8), clear span, imposed load, fire target (e.g. REI 60), acoustic targets (e.g. 45 dB airborne, 62 dB impact), finish, and preferred supply route.

**What they get back.** Each option against their incumbent slab, side by side: installed cost range per m², depth, embodied carbon, acoustic and fire status, buildability for local crews, named suppliers with grade and capacity, approval route by country, and the evidence grade of every number. A "what has to be true" panel lists the conditions still open for that option.

**Day-one content.** The Studio III pre-screen gives the first dataset. These are directional engineering estimates, not test data, and the tool must label them that way.

| Option | Depth (mm) | Cost vs beam-and-block | Airborne / impact (dB) | Embodied carbon (kg CO₂e/m²) | Evidence grade today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline: beam and block | ~240 | 1.00× | 50–55 / 58–65 | 110–140 | Market norm |
| 1 Timber joists with T&G | 275–300 | 0.90–1.10× | 42–48 / 70–80 (fails impact) | 20–30 | Engineering judgement |
| 2 Fabricated I-joist with topping | ~300 | 1.05–1.25× | 50–55 / 58–66 | 50–65 | Engineering judgement |
| 3 Timber cassette with topping | 245–270 | 1.10–1.35× | 52–57 / 55–62 | 55–70 | Engineering judgement; prototype candidate |
| 4 Cassette with strawboard layer | ~305 | 1.20–1.45× | 54–58 / 54–60 | 50–65 | Engineering judgement |
| 5 Cassette as permanent formwork | ~240 | 0.95–1.15× | 52–58 / 58–65 | 90–115 | Engineering judgement |
| 6 Cassette with timber soffit | ~185 | 1.15–1.40× | 48–54 / 60–68 | 40–55 | Needs prototype testing |
| 7 Timber-concrete composite | ~155 | 1.10–1.30× | 54–60 / 55–62 | 70–90 | Engineering judgement |

Source: Studio III performance requirements and floor options pack (draft), as summarised in the Bio-Based Construction Strategy Deck, September 2026. Design basis: 3 m span, 1.5 kN/m² imposed, REI 60, Kenya NBC 2024. The carbon unit is my reading of the deck's "kg" figures and should be confirmed with Studio III.

**How it grows across the portfolio.** Add ADHI's precast joist slab as a second incumbent, CPS's hollow-core timber box element, and CrossBoundary's lightweight extension floor. One floor module then compares substitution routes across Kenya, Rwanda and Zanzibar on a common basis, which is the cross-pilot comparability the MEL spine asks for.

**Second module.** Internal partitions for CrossBoundary's Mosaic conversions: non-structural, repeated on every floor, and still producing evidence if the vertical extension is a no-go.

## Running the economics honestly

The cost engine compares installed cost per m² against the user's own incumbent, by supply route, with ranges and named assumptions, and it never counts a benefit that nobody in the deal can capture.

```
ΔC = (M + P + L + D + S + T) − C_incumbent − verified building-level savings
```

M is material at grade (species, kiln drying, grading, treatment), P is secondary processing including imported adhesive, L is logistics by route, D is duties and border costs, S is site labour and lifting, and T is toppings and layers needed to hit fire and acoustic targets. Building-level savings (lighter frame or foundations, shorter programme) count only once an engineer or the developer's own cash-flow model confirms them.

Six rules keep it honest:

1. **Ranges, never points.** Each input carries low, central and high values plus its evidence grade. Sliders let a QS stress the inputs that move results most: exchange rate, timber price per m³, adhesive cost, haulage distance, and cement and steel prices.
2. **First-of-kind and repeat cost kept apart.** ADHI's BOQ already has to separate one-off R&D from repeatable unit cost; the tool applies that rule to every system and shows learning-curve gains as a scenario, not a promise.
3. **Route, not country.** Price Ugandan timber landed in Kigali, Zanzibar processing against imported finished elements, and regional kiln-dried timber against European imports. Exports outside the region appear only as a scenario tied to a named buyer.
4. **Who captures the benefit.** A panel states which party gains (developer, buyer, landlord, lender) and whether the deal structure lets them keep it. For example, a faster CPS build helps only if milestone payments let the buyer keep the gain.
5. **Carbon beside cost, not inside it.** Embodied carbon comes from the Bauhaus Earth LCAs, adjusted locally for grid, logistics and imported glue, in a format compatible with IFC's MAGC datasets. Biogenic storage is shown separately and never netted into cost, since EDGE V4 will assess upfront carbon only.
6. **Commercial sensitivity respected.** Partner cost data appears as anonymised ranges or medians unless the partner agrees otherwise. Developers can submit BOQs to enlarge the dataset, as CAHF invites practitioners to do.

The practical base is a standard reference BOQ per typology, built with a Kenyan QS on CAHF's method, so the bio-based option and the incumbent are priced in the same language a lender and a QS already use.

## Roadmap tied to the programme calendar

The build runs in step with the pilots so each module launches when its evidence arrives, and the tool is in users' hands a full year before the March 2028 replication gate.

Each phase ends with a go or no-go, in the same spirit as the pilot stop triggers:

1. **Discovery sprint.** Data schema and evidence grades, the seven floor options loaded, about ten user interviews, a clickable prototype. Go if at least three priority users say they would use it in a live decision.
2. **Floors module, closed beta.** Adds Timber World prototype results, a reference BOQ priced by a Kenyan QS, and the first Bauhaus Earth LCAs. Go if a developer uses it in a real specification decision.
3. **Partitions module.** Adds partitions for the Mosaic conversions plus the ADHI, CPS and CrossBoundary floor incumbents and supplier profiles.
4. **Evidence packs and demand view.** Finance and approval packs, aggregated demand for manufacturers, and alignment with EDGE V4, which IFC expects in the second half of 2027.
5. **Host and handover.** A host is agreed so the catalogue outlives programme funding, with data and governance transferred on a defined date.

## How we will know it works

The system succeeds when it causes decision events with suppliers, developers, lenders, valuers, insurers or regulators, which is the outcome language the MEL framework already uses; page views are a health check only.

| Indicator | Why it matters | Indicative target by March 2028 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specifications or tenders that cite a catalogue build-up | Shows the tool reached the spec list, the incremental pathway's real test | 3 or more, at least 1 from a non-partner developer |
| Supplier quotes issued against a catalogue spec | Shows manufacturers can price to a shared spec | 5 or more quotes from at least 2 suppliers |
| Evidence packs used by a lender, valuer or insurer in a live decision | Tests the pull engine on real paper | 2 or more |
| Approvals that reuse a catalogue dossier | Moves approval from exception to route | 1 or more in any country |
| BOQs and test results contributed by non-partners | Shows the data commons is alive beyond our grants | 5 or more |
| Systems shown as paused or stopped, with the binding constraint | Proves the tool publishes negative results | Every stopped pathway, within one quarter of the decision |

The targets are placeholders for the MEL team to calibrate against the portfolio scorecard (five systems baselined, one to two at the replication gate).

## Risks and guardrails

The biggest risk is false precision: a polished tool that makes unproven systems look settled, which would damage trust with exactly the engineers, lenders and regulators the pull engine needs.

| Risk | Guardrail |
| --- | --- |
| False precision or liability from design-like outputs | No structural design or construction outputs; BASE-style disclaimer; evidence grade on every number |
| Thin data makes the tool look empty | Launch closed beta with floors only; show gaps as open questions rather than hiding them |
| Partner commercial data leaks | Anonymised ranges by default; written consent per partner; no supplier prices shown without the supplier's agreement |
| Promoting a system that later fails | Status field (advancing, refining, paused, stopped) updated at each quarterly gate |
| Team time drawn away from pilots | Data entry built into existing pilot reporting; a contracted part-time product lead and QS, not core team time |
| Tool dies when the grant ends | Agree a host early (e.g. a university, a green building council or a professional body) with a defined exit, as the strategy requires for any facility |
| Duplicating EDGE or MAGC | Export to EDGE and MAGC formats; position the tool upstream of certification, never as an alternative |

## Decisions for the team

Six decisions would let the discovery sprint start in November; the first two could be raised at the 29 October TRM under "Refreshed strategy and learning framework".

- [ ] Is this a shared public good the programme funds (like testing, grading and LCA data), or a partner-led product? My recommendation is a public good with a named host and exit.
- [ ] Approve a two-month discovery sprint, with a product lead and a QS on contract, and a ceiling for it.
- [ ] Confirm floors as module one and partitions as module two, or name a different first module.
- [ ] Agree the data-sharing terms partners sign up to, starting with SHOUT/Zima, Studio III and Timber World.
- [ ] Decide whether bamboo and earth systems enter the catalogue now as "pilot first" entries, or only once a pilot exists. The regulatory review suggests they follow timber.
- [ ] Shortlist hosts to approach in 2027 and ask Bauhaus Earth whether the LCA work can feed the tool directly.
